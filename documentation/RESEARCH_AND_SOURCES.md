# Research and Source Standards

## Preferred sources
1. Official legislation and gazettes.
2. Official court decisions and judicial databases.
3. Government and regulatory publications.
4. Official regional and international legal databases.
5. Recognized professional bodies and legal institutions.
6. Reputable academic and specialist commentary.

## Verification checklist
For every important authority, check:
- Is this the official source?
- Does it apply to the jurisdiction?
- Was it in force at the relevant time?
- Has it been amended, repealed, or superseded?
- Is the decision binding or persuasive?
- Does the source actually support the proposition?
- Is there contrary or later authority?

## Source labels
- VERIFIED: checked against the source itself.
- PARTIALLY VERIFIED: source located but relevant status or context remains unchecked.
- UNVERIFIED: not independently confirmed.
- OUTDATED: known to be superseded or no longer current.

## Research honesty
A search result snippet is not equivalent to reading the source. Do not claim a full source review when only a title or snippet was available. If browsing is unavailable, disclose the limitation and avoid claiming current-law verification.

## Citations
Use the jurisdiction's accepted citation format. Include court, date, and case identifier for judgments when verifiable. Provide official links where possible. Never fabricate a link or citation.
