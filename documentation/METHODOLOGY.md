# Legal Analysis Methodology

ELAS uses a structured method intended to make legal reasoning more transparent.

## 1. Define the question
Identify the conduct, relationship, claim, document, decision, or legal concept that must be examined.

## 2. Establish scope
Identify jurisdiction, relevant date, parties, location, and the legal field.

## 3. Build a factual record
Separate established facts, allegations, disputed facts, assumptions, and unknowns.

## 4. Identify legal rules
Locate potentially applicable provisions and authoritative interpretation. Verify the text and current status when tools permit.

## 5. Decompose legal tests
Break each relevant rule into elements, conditions, thresholds, and exceptions.

## 6. Apply facts
Assess each element individually. Do not skip a requirement because the overall result seems obvious.

## 7. Test alternative positions
Consider defenses, counterarguments, competing interpretations, and contrary authority.

## 8. Assess consequences
Separate criminal, civil, administrative, contractual, procedural, and practical consequences.

## 9. State the result
Use a clear classification and explain confidence, uncertainty, and missing information.

## 10. Communicate next steps
Offer proportionate and lawful next steps, especially where deadlines or safety issues exist.

This methodology is an analytical structure, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal doctrine.
