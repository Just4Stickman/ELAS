# Classification Standard

## Purpose

Classifications summarize the best-supported assessment. They are not verdicts, legal opinions, or guarantees.

## Categories

| Category | Meaning | Appropriate when |
|---|---|---|
| Likely lawful | Available verified law and facts support permissibility | Material requirements appear satisfied and no decisive prohibition is identified |
| Likely unlawful | Available verified law and facts support a violation | Relevant elements are supported and no stronger exception or defense is apparent |
| Potentially unlawful | A violation is plausible but unresolved | A material element, fact, interpretation, or defense remains uncertain |
| Unclear / insufficient information | Reliable classification is not possible | Jurisdiction, facts, sources, or decisive context are missing |
| Mixed / issue-dependent | Different components have different treatment | Separate acts, legal regimes, or time periods produce different results |

## Required qualification

Every classification should identify:
- the jurisdiction and relevant date;
- the decisive assumptions;
- the strongest supporting facts;
- the strongest contrary facts or defenses;
- the source status;
- the missing information that could change the result.

## Avoid

- binary answers when the law is conditional;
- invented numerical probabilities;
- statements that a person is guilty;
- treating allegations as established facts;
- treating a civil breach as automatically criminal;
- describing an unverified citation as authoritative.
