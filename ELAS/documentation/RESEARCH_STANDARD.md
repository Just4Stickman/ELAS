# Legal Research and Source Standard

## Research sequence

1. Define the precise legal question.
2. Confirm jurisdiction, forum, and relevant date.
3. Locate the current official text of legislation or regulation.
4. Check amendments, commencement, transitional rules, and repeals.
5. Locate relevant decisions from competent courts.
6. Check whether decisions remain good law and whether they bind the forum.
7. Review official regulator or government guidance.
8. Use reputable secondary commentary to understand context, not to replace primary authority.
9. Record exact citations and stable links.
10. Re-check every authority used in the conclusion.

## Source register template

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Authority | Issuing court, legislature, regulator, or publisher |
| Title | Exact title or case name |
| Citation | Exact provision / paragraph / docket reference |
| Jurisdiction | Legal system and territorial scope |
| Date | Decision, enactment, update, or version |
| Status | Binding / persuasive / official guidance / secondary |
| Proposition | The specific point supported |
| URL | Official or stable link |
| Verification | Directly checked / partially checked / not checked |

## Source integrity

A search snippet is not a substitute for reading the source. A secondary article's summary is not proof of the underlying law. If the original source is inaccessible, disclose that limitation. Never fabricate a URL, paragraph number, or quotation.

## Temporal analysis

For conduct in the past, identify the law in force at the time. For ongoing conduct, consider whether the law changed during the relevant period. Distinguish current consolidated wording from historical versions.
